HubSpot Too Much? Simpler CRM Alternatives for Small Teams

4 August 2026 · ~7 min read

HubSpot is a genuinely excellent product. It's also built for a company that looks nothing like a five-person sales team — and that mismatch is why a lot of small teams end up frustrated with it, not because they're using it wrong, but because they're using something designed for someone else.

If you've found yourself paying for modules you never open, or clicking through three screens to do something that should take one, this is for you. Here's how to tell when HubSpot is the wrong fit, what a simpler alternative looks like, and how to move without losing anything.

HubSpot isn't bad — it's big

The thing to be clear about: HubSpot's breadth is a feature, not a flaw. Marketing automation, service desks, CMS, operations tooling, deep reporting — if you'll use most of that, it's hard to beat. The problem is that breadth has a cost in two currencies: money and attention.

The free tier is famously generous, which is how most small teams start. But the features that made you want HubSpot in the first place — automation, sequences, the good reporting — tend to sit in the paid tiers, and the price steps up quickly as you add seats and unlock them. Meanwhile, every day, your team navigates an interface built to house all of that, whether they use it or not.

Signs you've outgrown it in the wrong direction

Most teams don't outgrow HubSpot by getting too big for it. They outgrow it by realising they were never the right size for it. A few signs:

  • You use the CRM (contacts, deals) and almost none of the marketing or service tools you're paying toward.
  • Onboarding a new team member means a training session, not an afternoon.
  • Simple daily actions — log a call, move a deal, see who to chase — feel like more clicks than they should.
  • Your bill has climbed past what a small sales team can comfortably justify for "a place to track deals".

None of those mean HubSpot is bad. They mean you're paying for a platform when what you need is a CRM.

What a simpler alternative looks like

When small teams move off HubSpot for something lighter, the shortlist usually falls into two camps: a pipeline-first CRM (Pipedrive being the best-known), or a focused, all-in-the-core CRM built specifically for small teams. We've written a fuller breakdown in our guide to choosing the best CRM for a small sales team, but the criteria that matter most after leaving a big platform are:

Speed over surface area. How fast can you log a call or move a deal? Usable in an afternoon, not a rollout. Priced in plain terms, so you know what a seat costs without a sales call. And only the features you'll actually use — the point of leaving is to stop paying for and navigating things you don't touch.

Moving without losing your data

The fear that keeps teams on the wrong tool is usually migration. In practice, moving a small team's CRM is far less dramatic than it sounds:

Export your contacts and deals. HubSpot lets you export to CSV. That's the bulk of what matters — your people and your open opportunities.

Import into the new tool and rebuild your pipeline. For a small team this is an afternoon, not a project. Your pipeline stages are a handful of columns; your contacts are a spreadsheet.

Run both in parallel for a fortnight. Have the team log real activity in the new tool while HubSpot stays as a safety net. If the new one fits your working day, the data builds up and you switch off the old one. If it doesn't, you've lost nothing.

The honest test

Don't switch to save money alone — switch because the tool fits how your team actually works. The question isn't "is HubSpot good?" (it is). It's "am I paying for, and working around, a platform when I need a CRM?" If the answer is yes, a simpler tool won't feel like a downgrade. It'll feel like someone finally built the thing for you.
