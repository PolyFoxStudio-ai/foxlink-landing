Affordable CRM for Small Business: What £19 a Seat Actually Gets You

18 August 2026 · ~7 min read

"Affordable" and "cheap" get used interchangeably in CRM, but they're not the same thing. Cheap is a small number on a pricing page. Affordable is a tool that's worth what you pay for it and doesn't surprise you later. Plenty of cheap CRMs turn out to be expensive once you add up everything the low headline number left out.

So before you pick on price, it's worth understanding what actually drives CRM pricing, where the hidden costs live, and what a fair per-seat price should include.

Why the free tier isn't really free

Almost every CRM offers a free plan, and for a solo operator it can genuinely be enough. But free tiers are a starting point, not a destination — they're deliberately shaped so that the features a growing team needs (more contacts, automations, reporting, the AI bits) sit just over the line in a paid plan. That's a fair trade, but it means "free" often becomes "£X per seat" faster than you planned, and the jump can be steep.

The honest way to price a CRM isn't the free tier — it's the plan you'll actually be on in three months, times the seats you'll actually have.

Where the hidden costs hide

The headline per-seat price is rarely the whole bill. Watch for:

  • Feature gating. The things that made you choose the CRM — automation, good reporting, AI assistance — parked in a higher tier.
  • Add-ons. Charges for extra contacts, extra pipelines, or capabilities sold separately from the base seat.
  • Currency risk. Per-seat pricing in dollars that drifts with the exchange rate, so your UK bill moves for reasons that have nothing to do with your business.
  • Setup and time. The least visible cost of all: if a CRM takes weeks to configure or a training session to learn, that's real money in hours — and often the reason a "cheap" CRM ends up abandoned.

The real cost of a CRM = (price per seat × seats you'll have) + add-ons + the hours it takes to set up and learn. A tool that's £5 cheaper per seat but needs two weeks of setup isn't cheaper.

What a fair £19/seat should include

For a small sales team, a fair mid-range price should get you the whole core — not a stripped base you have to upgrade out of. Specifically:

  • A full deal pipeline, contacts and companies — with no cap that forces an upgrade the moment you grow.
  • Fast activity logging, tasks and a calendar — the daily tools, included.
  • The useful AI, in the base plan — lead scoring, follow-up help, note cleaning — not as a premium tease.
  • Clear, single-currency pricing and no per-feature nickel-and-diming.

How to compare honestly

Line up two or three options and, for each, work out the true monthly cost for your team on the plan that has the features you need — not the free tier, not the headline. Add the setup time. Then run a real two-week trial with real data (see our guide to choosing the best CRM for a small sales team). The cheapest tool that your team actually uses every day is almost always the most affordable one — because a CRM nobody uses is 100% wasted spend, whatever the sticker said.
